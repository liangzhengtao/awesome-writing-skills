# Grant Proposal Writing

> Write grant proposals that convince reviewers to fund your research.

## When to Use

- Writing a new grant proposal (NSF, NIH, ERC, etc.)
- Revising a previously rejected proposal
- Preparing a letter of intent or pre-proposal
- Writing a research statement for fellowship applications
- Crafting the specific aims page for an NIH application
- Developing a project description for NSF

---

## Writing Framework

### Step 1: Understand the Funder

Before writing, research your target funder:

| Question | Where to Find It |
|----------|------------------|
| What do they fund? | Program description, funded project database |
| What's the review criteria? | Solicitation, reviewer guidelines |
| What's the success rate? | NSF/NIH statistics, program officer conversations |
| What's the budget range? | Solicitation, recent awards |
| Who reviews proposals? | Study section composition (NIH), panel descriptions (NSF) |

### Step 2: Core Sections

Most proposals include these sections (names vary by funder):

```
1. PROJECT SUMMARY / ABSTRACT
   └── 300-500 words covering the entire proposal

2. SPECIFIC AIMS / OBJECTIVES
   └── 1 page — the most important page of the proposal

3. SIGNIFICANCE / BACKGROUND
   └── Why this research matters

4. INNOVATION / ORIGINALITY
   └── What's new and different about this approach

5. APPROACH / METHODOLOGY
   └── How you'll do it (the longest section)

6. TIMELINE / MILESTONES
   └── When you'll do it

7. BUDGET JUSTIFICATION
   └── Why you need the money

8. REFERENCES
   └── Supporting literature
```

### Step 3: The Specific Aims Page

This is the single most important page. Structure it as:

```
Opening Hook (2-3 sentences)
├── State the big problem/need
├── Provide the key statistic or evidence
└── Establish urgency

Gap Statement (2-3 sentences)
├── What current approaches can't do
├── What we don't know
└── What's missing

Long-Term Goal (1 sentence)
└── Your overarching research vision

This Proposal's Aim (1 sentence)
└── What THIS project will accomplish

Specific Aims (numbered list)
├── Aim 1: [Action verb] [what] [how] [to achieve what]
├── Aim 2: [Action verb] [what] [how] [to achieve what]
└── Aim 3: [Action verb] [what] [how] [to achieve what]

Impact Statement (1-2 sentences)
└── What changes if this succeeds
```

### Step 4: The "So What?" Test

For every section, ask: "So what? Why should the reviewer care?"

| Section | "So What?" Question |
|---------|-------------------|
| Background | "So what gap does this leave?" |
| Significance | "So what happens if we don't solve this?" |
| Innovation | "So what's different from existing work?" |
| Approach | "So what makes you confident this will work?" |
| Impact | "So what changes in the real world?" |

### Step 5: Reviewer Psychology

Reviewers read dozens of proposals. Make theirs easy:

- **First paragraph:** Hook them with the problem
- **First page:** Make your aims crystal clear
- **Headings and subheadings:** Guide them through your logic
- **Figures and diagrams:** Break up text, illustrate complex ideas
- **Bold key phrases:** Help scanners find the important parts
- **Parallel structure:** Use consistent formatting for aims and milestones

### Broader Impacts

- [Finding 1] [Reference]

### Template 2: NIH Specific Aims Page

```markdown
## SPECIFIC AIMS

[Opening: 2-3 sentences establishing the disease/condition and its
clinical significance. Include a compelling statistic.]

Current [treatments/approaches/diagnostics] for [condition] are limited by
[specific limitation]. [1-2 sentences on why existing approaches fail.]

**Long-term goal:** Our long-term research program aims to [overarching vision].

**Central hypothesis:** We hypothesize that [testable hypothesis], which
will [expected impact on the field].

**Rationale:** This hypothesis is supported by our preliminary data showing
[brief summary of key preliminary findings].

To test this hypothesis, we propose the following specific aims:

**Aim 1: [Action verb] [what] [to determine/characterize/test what].**
[1-2 sentences on approach]
[Milestone: key deliverable or expected outcome]

**Aim 2: [Action verb] [what] [to determine/characterize/test what].**
[1-2 sentences on approach]
[Milestone: key deliverable or expected outcome]

**Aim 3: [Action verb] [what] [to determine/characterize/test what].**
[1-2 sentences on approach]
[Milestone: key deliverable or expected outcome]

**Impact:** Successful completion of these aims will [specific impact on
the field, clinical practice, or public health]. This work is significant
because [1-2 sentences on broader significance].

---

### Aim Verb Bank

Use precise action verbs:

| Category | Verbs |
|----------|-------|
| Discovery | determine, identify, characterize, discover, establish |
| Development | develop, create, design, optimize, validate |
| Testing | test, evaluate, assess, compare, measure |
| Mechanistic | elucidate, investigate, define, delineate |
```

## Power Words and Phrases

### Opening/Hook Phrases
| Category | Phrases |
|----------|---------|
| Urgency | "Affects X million people annually...", "Costs $X billion per year..." |
| Gap | "Despite significant progress, a critical gap remains in..." |
| Opportunity | "Recent advances in X create an unprecedented opportunity to..." |
| Problem | "X remains a major challenge in [field]..." |

### Significance Phrases
- "This project addresses a critical need in..."
- "The proposed research will fundamentally advance our understanding of..."
- "Success will directly benefit [population/field] by..."
- "This work fills a significant gap in..."
- "The expected outcomes will have lasting impact on..."

### Innovation Phrases
- "Unlike existing approaches, this proposal..."
- "This represents a paradigm shift from... to..."
- "For the first time, we will..."
- "This novel approach overcomes the key limitation of..."
- "We introduce a fundamentally new framework for..."

### Approach Phrases
- "We will test this hypothesis using..."
- "Our preliminary data demonstrate..."
- "This approach is feasible because..."
- "We have successfully used this method to..."
- "Risk mitigation: If [risk], we will [alternative approach]."

### Impact Phrases
- "These findings will enable..."
- "The tools developed will be broadly applicable to..."
- "This work will train the next generation of..."
- "Results will be disseminated through [channels]."

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Being Too Ambitious
❌ Proposing 10 aims that would take a career to complete.

✅ 3 focused aims that build on each other and are achievable in the
proposed timeframe.

### 2. Weak Specific Aims Page
❌ Burying the aims in dense text on page 5.

✅ Specific aims on page 1, crystal clear, with parallel structure.

### 3. No Preliminary Data
❌ "We will investigate X for the first time" with no evidence you can.

✅ "Our preliminary data show [result], supporting the feasibility of
this approach" with figures/tables.

### 4. Ignoring the Review Criteria
❌ Writing a great proposal that doesn't address what reviewers are scored on.

✅ Map every section to a review criterion. Use the same language.

### 5. Too Much Background, Not Enough Approach
❌ 8 pages of literature review, 2 pages of methodology.

✅ 3-4 pages background, 8-10 pages of detailed, feasible methodology.

### 6. No Risk Mitigation
❌ "We will use method X" without addressing what happens if X doesn't work.

✅ "We will use method X. If X proves insufficient, we will fall back on
method Y, which we have validated in [preliminary data]."

### 7. Generic Impact Statements
❌ "This research will advance knowledge in the field."

✅ "This research will produce a validated diagnostic tool that can be
deployed in clinical settings within 3 years, directly benefiting the
2.1 million patients diagnosed with [condition] annually."

## Before / After Examples

### Example 1: Opening Hook

**Before (Weak):**
> Cancer is a serious disease that affects many people. There are many
> types of cancer. Research is important for finding new treatments.

**After (Strong):**
> Pancreatic cancer has a 5-year survival rate of just 12% — the lowest
> of all major cancers. Despite decades of research, early detection
> remains elusive: 80% of patients are diagnosed at advanced stages when
> surgical intervention is no longer viable. A reliable blood-based
> biomarker panel could shift this paradigm by enabling screening in
> high-risk populations.

### Example 2: Specific Aim

**Before (Vague):**
> Aim 1: Study the effects of the new compound on cancer cells.

**After (Precise):**
> **Aim 1:** Determine the efficacy of compound XBR-429 in reducing tumor
> growth in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models of pancreatic ductal
> adenocarcinoma (PDAC). We will treat N=30 PDX-bearing mice with XBR-429
> (three dose levels) or vehicle control over 8 weeks, measuring tumor
> volume weekly by caliper and bioluminescence imaging. Success criterion:
> ≥40% reduction in tumor volume at the highest dose compared to vehicle
> (α=0.05, power=0.80).

### Example 3: Innovation Statement

**Before (Generic):**
> This project is innovative because it uses a new method.

**After (Specific):**
> This project is innovative in three ways: (1) It is the first to apply
> single-cell spatial transcriptomics to the tumor microenvironment in
> PDAC, enabling cell-type-specific analysis of the immune landscape;
> (2) It integrates multi-omic data using a novel computational framework
> (developed in our lab) that resolves cell-cell communication networks;
> (3) It bridges the gap between basic discovery and clinical application
> by validating biomarker candidates in a prospective clinical cohort.

## 中文版本

### 使用场景

- 撰写新基金申请书（NSF、NIH、ERC等）
- 修改被拒的申请书
- 准备意向书或预申请
- 为奖学金申请撰写研究陈述
- 撰写NIH的Specific Aims页面
- 开发NSF项目描述

### 写作框架

**第一步：了解资助方** — 研究资助方的资助范围、评审标准、成功率、预算范围、评审人员构成。

**第二步：核心章节** — 项目摘要（300-500词）→ Specific Aims/目标（最重要的1页）→ 意义/背景 → 创新性 → 方法（最长部分）→ 时间线 → 预算说明 → 参考文献。

**第三步：Specific Aims页面** — 开头钩子（大问题+关键数据+紧迫性）→ 空白陈述 → 长期目标 → 本提案目标 → 具体目标（3个，用精确动词开头）→ 影响声明。

**第四步："那又怎样？"测试** — 每个章节都问："评审为什么要关心？"背景→留下了什么空白？意义→不解决会怎样？创新→与现有工作有何不同？

**第五步：评审心理** — 评审阅读大量申请书，要让他们轻松：第一段用问题吸引、第一页目标清晰、善用标题和图表、加粗关键短语、保持结构平行。

### 模板说明

- **NSF提案模板**：含项目摘要（概述+学术价值+广泛影响）→ 项目描述（15页：背景→目标→方法→时间线→广泛影响）
- **NIH Specific Aims模板**：含开场→当前局限→长期目标→核心假设→依据→3个具体目标→影响声明，附Aim动词库
- **ERC提案模板**：含概要（5页）+ 扩展概要（15页：科学背景→方法→影响→与工作计划的契合）

### 常见错误

1. **目标过于宏大** — 3个聚焦且相互支撑的目标，而非10个需要用一辈子完成的
2. **Specific Aims页面无力** — 目标必须在第1页，结构清晰，平行排列
3. **无预研数据** — "首次研究X"不如"预研数据表明[结果]，支持可行性"
4. **忽视评审标准** — 每个章节对标评审标准，使用相同的语言
5. **背景过多，方法过少** — 背景3-4页，方法8-10页详细可行的方案
6. **无风险应对** — "使用方法X"应补充"若X不够，备选方法Y（已验证）"
7. **影响声明空泛** — "推进知识"不如"3年内可在临床部署的诊断工具，惠及210万患者"
