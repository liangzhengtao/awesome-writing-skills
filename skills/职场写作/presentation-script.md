# Presentation Script Writing

> Write scripts that hold attention, convey ideas clearly, and drive action.

## When to Use

- Writing a pitch deck script for investors
- Preparing a conference talk or keynote
- Creating a script for a team meeting or update
- Writing narration for a webinar or online presentation
- Preparing a product demo script
- Crafting a TED-style talk

---

## Writing Framework

### Step 1: Opening Hooks (First 60 Seconds)

You have 60 seconds to earn the next 30 minutes.

| Hook Type | Example | Best For |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Story | "Three years ago, I was sitting in a hospital waiting room..." | Emotional connection |
| Shocking stat | "73% of the audience will forget everything I say today. Here's how to be in the 27%." | Data-driven talks |
| Question | "What if I told you that everything you know about [topic] is wrong?" | Thought leadership |
| Bold claim | "By the end of this talk, you'll have a framework that will save you 10 hours a week." | Practical talks |
| Contrast | "Two years ago, we were 6 months from bankruptcy. Today, we're the fastest-growing company in our sector." | Case studies |

### Step 2: Story Structure for Presentations

Every great presentation follows a narrative arc:

```
OPENING (10% of time)
├── Hook: Grab attention
├── Credibility: Why should they listen to you?
└── Road map: What you'll cover

ACT 1: THE PROBLEM (20% of time)
├── Establish the status quo
├── Show what's broken
└── Create tension (why the status quo can't continue)

ACT 2: THE SOLUTION (40% of time)
├── Introduce your key idea (one core message)
├── Evidence point 1 (data, story, or demo)
├── Evidence point 2 (data, story, or demo)
├── Evidence point 3 (data, story, or demo)
└── Each point builds on the previous

ACT 3: THE APPLICATION (20% of time)
├── What this means for the audience
├── Practical next steps
└── Address the "how" question

CLOSING (10% of time)
├── Recap the core message
├── Call to action
└── Memorable final line
```

### Step 3: Data Narration

Don't just present data — tell its story.

| Step | What to Do | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| 1. Set up | Tell them what to look for | "Watch what happens to the blue line after Q2." |
| 2. Reveal | Show the data | [Display the chart] |
| 3. Interpret | Explain what it means | "That's a 40% increase — and it's accelerating." |
| 4. Connect | Link to their world | "For a 100-person team, that's 400 hours saved per month." |
| 5. Imply | Suggest the next step | "The question isn't whether to adopt this. It's how fast." |

### Step 4: Slide-by-Slide Script Format

For each slide, write:

```
SLIDE: [Slide title]
VISUAL: [What's on the screen]

SCRIPT:
[Exact words to say — conversational, not reading from the slide]

TRANSITION:
[How you bridge to the next slide]

NOTES:
[Pacing, emphasis, gestures, pauses]
```

### Step 5: Pacing and Delivery Notes

| Element | Guideline |
|---------|-----------|
| Pace | 130-150 words per minute (slightly slower than conversation) |
| Pauses | After key points (2-3 seconds), after questions (3-5 seconds) |
| Emphasis | Bold or CAPS for words to stress |
| Movement | Note when to walk, gesture, or stand still |
| Eye contact | Note which section of the audience to address |

### Step 6: Closing with Impact

Your closing is what they'll remember. Don't waste it.

| Closing Type | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| Callback | Reference your opening story — but with a new understanding |
| Challenge | "Here's my challenge to you..." |
| Vision | Paint a picture of the future you're proposing |
| Question | Leave them with something to think about |
| Quote | Only if it's perfect and adds genuine value |

**Never end with:** "That's all I have" or "Any questions?" as your final line.
Have a prepared closing statement, THEN open for Q&A.

## Templates

### Template 1: Pitch Deck Script (10 Minutes)

```markdown
# [Company/Product] Pitch Script

**Total time:** 10 minutes
**Slides:** 10-12
**Audience:** [Investors/Partners/Customers]

---

## Slide 1: Title
**Visual:** Company logo + tagline

**Script:**
"Thank you for your time today. I'm [Name], [Title] of [Company].
In the next 10 minutes, I'm going to show you how we're going to
[big promise]."

---

## Slide 2: The Problem
**Visual:** [Image or stat representing the problem]

**Script:**
"Let me start with a problem that [target audience] face every day.

[Personal story or specific example — 30 seconds]

Right now, they're solving this by [current solution]. The problem?
[Specific limitation of current solution].

This costs [target audience] [specific metric — time, money, frustration]."

**Transition:** "What if there were a better way?"

---

## Slide 3: The Solution
**Visual:** Product screenshot or demo

**Script:**
"This is [Product Name].

In one sentence: [What it does, in plain language].

Here's how it works:

[Walk through the key workflow — 60 seconds]

See how [specific benefit]?"

**Transition:** "But don't take my word for it."

---

## Slide 4: Traction / Social Proof
**Visual:** Key metrics, logos, or testimonial quotes

**Script:**
"In [timeframe since launch], here's what we've achieved:

- [Metric 1]: [Number]
- [Metric 2]: [Number]
- [Metric 3]: [Number]

Our customers include [notable names]. Here's what they say:

> '[Short, powerful testimonial]' — [Name, Company]"

**Transition:** "Let me show you how we did this."

---

## Slide 5: How It Works / Technology
**Visual:** Architecture diagram or process flow

**Script:**
"[Product] is built on [key technology/approach].

What makes us different is [unique differentiator].

[Explain the key technical insight in simple terms]

This gives us [competitive advantage — faster, cheaper, more accurate]."

**Transition:** "So how big is the opportunity?"

---

## Slide 6: Market Opportunity
**Visual:** Market size diagram (TAM/SAM/SOM)

**Script:**
"The total addressable market is $[X]B.

Our serviceable market is $[Y]B — [brief explanation of scope].

We're initially targeting [specific niche], which is $[Z]B.

This market is growing at [X]% annually because [key trend]."

**Transition:** "Here's how we capture it."

---

## Slide 7: Business Model
**Visual:** Revenue model diagram

**Script:**
"We make money through [model — subscription, transaction, etc.].

Our average customer pays $[X] per [period].
Customer acquisition cost is $[X].
Lifetime value is $[Y].
LTV:CAC ratio is [Z]:1.

[If applicable: secondary revenue streams]"

**Transition:** "Let me show you our roadmap."

---

## Slide 8: Go-to-Market Strategy
**Visual:** Timeline or funnel

**Script:**
"Phase 1 ([timeframe]): [Strategy] — targeting [segment]
Phase 2 ([timeframe]): [Strategy] — expanding to [segment]
Phase 3 ([timeframe]): [Strategy] — [scale play]

Our unfair advantage in distribution is [specific insight or asset]."

**Transition:** "Now, let me introduce the team."

---

## Slide 9: Team
**Visual:** Team photos and logos

**Script:**
"We have [X] people, [Y] of whom are technical.

I'm [Name] — [one sentence credibility marker].

Our CTO, [Name], [credibility marker].

Our [key hire], [Name], [credibility marker].

We've worked together at [previous company] where we [relevant achievement]."

**Transition:** "Here's what we're asking for."

---

## Slide 10: The Ask
**Visual:** Funding amount, use of funds breakdown

**Script:**
"We're raising $[X] to [specific use].

Here's how we'll use it:
- [Category 1]: $X (Y%)
- [Category 2]: $X (Y%)
- [Category 3]: $X (Y%)

With this funding, we'll achieve [specific milestone] by [date].

In [timeframe], we project [revenue/growth metric].

I'd love to discuss how we can work together."

**Closing:**
"Thank you. I'm [Name], and I believe [one-sentence conviction statement].
I'd love to take your questions."
```

### Template 2: Conference Talk Script (20 Minutes)

```markdown
# [Talk Title]

**Duration:** 20 minutes + 5 Q&A
**Audience:** [Description]
**Key takeaway:** [One sentence — what should they remember?]

---

## Opening (2 minutes)

**[Slide 1: Title slide]**

"[Hook — personal story, surprising stat, or provocative question]

[Establish credibility — why are YOU talking about this?]

Today, I'm going to share [core promise]. You'll walk away with
[actionable framework/approach/insight].

Here's the roadmap: We'll start with [Part 1], then [Part 2],
and finish with [Part 3]."

---

## Part 1: The Problem (5 minutes)

**[Slides 2-4]**

"[Establish the context — what's the current state?]

[Show what's broken — use data or stories]

Here's the key insight most people miss:
[Your unique perspective]

And this matters because [consequence of not solving it].

[Supporting story or case study — 1 minute]"

---

## Part 2: The Framework (8 minutes)

**[Slides 5-10]**

"So how do we solve this? I've developed a [framework/method/approach]
called [Name]. It has [number] steps.

**Step 1: [Name]**
[Explanation — 1-2 minutes]
[Example or demo]
[Key insight]

**Step 2: [Name]**
[Explanation — 1-2 minutes]
[Example or demo]
[Key insight]

**Step 3: [Name]**
[Explanation — 1-2 minutes]
[Example or demo]
[Key insight]

[If time allows: case study showing all 3 steps in action]"

---

## Part 3: Application (3 minutes)

**[Slides 11-12]**

"Now, here's how YOU can apply this starting tomorrow:

**If you're a [persona 1]:** [Specific advice]
**If you're a [persona 2]:** [Specific advice]
**If you're a [persona 3]:** [Specific advice]

The biggest mistake people make when implementing this is [mistake].
Here's how to avoid it: [solution]."

---

## Closing (2 minutes)

**[Slide 13: Key takeaway + contact info]**

"[Callback to opening story — but now they understand the resolution]

Let me leave you with this:

[Memorable final statement — your core message in one sentence]

[If applicable: specific challenge or call to action]

Thank you."

---

## Q&A Notes (5 minutes)

**Anticipated questions and prepared answers:**

Q1: [Likely question]
A: [Prepared answer]

Q2: [Likely question]
A: [Prepared answer]

Q3: [Likely question]
A: [Prepared answer]

**If you don't know the answer:**
"That's a great question. I don't have the data to answer that
precisely, but here's my thinking: [framework for answering].
I'd love to follow up with you after with specifics."
```

### Template 3: Team Update Script (5 Minutes)

```markdown
# [Team/Project] Update — [Date]

**Duration:** 5 minutes
**Audience:** [Team/Department/Company]

---

## Opening (30 seconds)

"Hey everyone, quick update on [project/team]. I'll cover three things:
what we shipped, what's next, and where we need help."

---

## What We Shipped (2 minutes)

"Last [week/sprint], we accomplished:

**1. [Achievement 1]**
[One sentence context + result]

**2. [Achievement 2]**
[One sentence context + result]

**3. [Achievement 3]**
[One sentence context + result]

Shout-out to [Name] for [specific contribution]."

---

## What's Next (1.5 minutes)

"This [week/sprint], we're focused on:

**Priority 1: [Task]** — Owner: [Name] — Due: [Date]
[One sentence on why it matters]

**Priority 2: [Task]** — Owner: [Name] — Due: [Date]
[One sentence on why it matters]

**Priority 3: [Task]** — Owner: [Name] — Due: [Date]
[One sentence on why it matters]"

---

## Where We Need Help (30 seconds)

"We're blocked on [specific issue]. If you can help with [specific ask],
please reach out to [Name] by [date]."

---

## Closing (30 seconds)

"That's the update. Questions?

[If no questions:] Great. Let's have a productive [week/sprint]."
```

## Power Words and Phrases

### Opening Phrases
- "I want to start with a story..."
- "Here's a number that should keep you up at night..."
- "I've been thinking about this for [timeframe]..."
- "What I'm about to share changed how I think about [topic]."
- "Raise your hand if [relatable question]..."

### Transition Phrases
- "Which brings me to my next point..."
- "Now, here's where it gets interesting..."
- "Let me show you what I mean..."
- "Stay with me here..."
- "This is the part I'm most excited about..."

### Emphasis Phrases
- "Let me say that again."
- "This is the key insight."
- "If you remember one thing from today, remember this:"
- "Here's the bottom line:"
- "The most important thing to understand is..."

### Closing Phrases
- "Let me bring it all together..."
- "Here's my challenge to each of you..."
- "I started today with [callback to opening]. Now you know the rest of the story."
- "The question isn't [common objection]. The question is [reframe]."
- "Thank you. I'm [Name]. And I believe [conviction statement]."

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Reading Slides Aloud
❌ Slides full of text, presenter reads every word.

✅ Slides have visuals and keywords. YOU provide the narrative.

### 2. No Story or Narrative
❌ A data dump with no through-line.

✅ Every presentation is a story: problem → solution → call to action.

### 3. Too Much Content
❌ Trying to cover everything in 20 minutes.

✅ One core message, supported by 3 key points. That's it.

### 4. Weak Opening
❌ "Hi, I'm [Name], and today I'll be talking about [topic]."

✅ Start with a hook. Earn their attention before asking for their time.

### 5. No Clear Close
❌ "So... yeah. That's it. Any questions?"

✅ End with a deliberate, prepared closing statement. THEN take questions.

### 6. Ignoring the Audience
❌ Writing for yourself instead of for them.

✅ Every point should answer: "Why should THEY care?"

### 7. No Rehearsal
❌ First time saying the words is on stage.

✅ Practice aloud at least 3 times. Time yourself. Record and review.

## Before / After Examples

### Example 1: Opening

**Before:**
> Good morning, everyone. My name is Sarah, and I'm the VP of Product
> at Acme Corp. Today I'll be talking about our approach to customer
> retention. I'll cover our strategy, some metrics, and our plans
> for next year.

**After:**
> Six months ago, we lost our biggest customer.
>
> $2 million in annual revenue — gone. And the worst part? They didn't
> leave because of our product. They left because we stopped paying
> attention.
>
> That moment forced us to rethink everything we knew about keeping
> customers. Today, I'm going to share what we learned — and the
> framework that reduced our churn by 40%.

### Example 2: Data Presentation

**Before:**
> As you can see from this chart, our revenue increased from $3.2M in
> Q1 to $4.1M in Q2, representing a growth rate of approximately 28%.
> This was primarily driven by expansion in the enterprise segment.

**After:**
> [Show chart]
>
> See that jump? That's $900,000 in one quarter.
>
> But here's the interesting part — it wasn't spread evenly.
> 80% of that growth came from just 5 enterprise accounts.
>
> Five customers. Nearly a million dollars.
>
> Which raises an obvious question: what would happen if we had
> 50 accounts like that?

### Example 3: Closing

**Before:**
> So yeah, that's our retention strategy. I hope you found it useful.
> Any questions?

**After:**
> Let me bring it back to where we started.
>
> That customer we lost six months ago? They're back.
>
> Not because of a discount. Not because of a sales pitch. Because
> we built a system that notices when customers are slipping — before
> they decide to leave.
>
> My challenge to you: look at your own customer base this week.
> Find the ones going quiet. Reach out before it's too late.
>
> Thank you.
