# Business Report Writing

> Write business reports that inform decisions and drive action.

## When to Use

- Writing a quarterly or annual business report
- Creating a project proposal or business case
- Preparing a risk assessment document
- Writing a competitive analysis report
- Summarizing research findings for stakeholders
- Creating an operational review

---

## Writing Framework

### Step 1: Know Your Audience

| Audience | What They Want | How to Write |
|----------|---------------|--------------|
| C-Suite | Bottom line, decisions needed | Lead with conclusions, 1-2 pages max |
| Directors | Strategic implications | Balance detail and summary |
| Managers | Actionable details | Full context with clear recommendations |
| Technical team | Complete data and methodology | Comprehensive, precise, detailed |

### Step 2: The Pyramid Principle

Lead with the answer, then support it:

```
CONCLUSION / RECOMMENDATION (Start here)
│
├── Key Finding 1
│   ├── Supporting data
│   └── Supporting data
│
├── Key Finding 2
│   ├── Supporting data
│   └── Supporting data
│
└── Key Finding 3
    ├── Supporting data
    └── Supporting data
```

**Why this works:** Executives read the first page. If the answer is buried on page 12, they never see it.

### Step 3: Report Structure

```
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1 page)
   ├── Key findings (3-5 bullets)
   ├── Main recommendation
   └── Expected impact / ROI

2. INTRODUCTION (1 page)
   ├── Purpose and scope
   ├── Methodology
   └── Key terms defined

3. FINDINGS / ANALYSIS (Bulk of the report)
   ├── Finding 1: Data + Analysis + Implication
   ├── Finding 2: Data + Analysis + Implication
   └── Finding 3: Data + Analysis + Implication

4. DISCUSSION (1-2 pages)
   ├── Interpretation of findings
   ├── Limitations
   └── Unexpected results

5. RECOMMENDATIONS (1 page)
   ├── Specific, actionable recommendations
   ├── Prioritized (high/medium/low impact)
   └── Timeline and responsible parties

6. APPENDICES
   ├── Detailed data tables
   ├── Methodology notes
   └── Supporting documents
```

### Step 4: Data Presentation

| Data Type | Best Format | Rules |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Trends over time | Line chart | Label axes, highlight key points |
| Comparisons | Bar chart or table | Sort by value, highlight differences |
| Proportions | Pie chart (≤5 segments) or stacked bar | Label percentages |
| Relationships | Scatter plot | Include trendline and R² |
| Detailed data | Table | Align numbers right, bold totals |

**Table Rules:**
- Use consistent decimal places
- Right-align numbers
- Bold totals and key figures
- Include units in headers, not every cell
- Add source and date in footnotes

### Step 5: Recommendation Format

Every recommendation must be:

```
SPECIFIC — What exactly should be done?
MEASURABLE — How will we know it worked?
ACHIEVABLE — Can we actually do this?
RELEVANT — Does it address the finding?
TIME-BOUND — When should it be done?
```

**Format:**
```
Recommendation: [What to do]
Rationale: [Why, based on findings]
Expected Impact: [Quantified benefit]
Timeline: [When]
Owner: [Who is responsible]
Cost: [Estimated investment]
```

## Power Words and Phrases

### Executive Summary Phrases
| Category | Phrases |
|----------|---------|
| Findings | "Analysis reveals...", "Data indicates...", "The evidence shows..." |
| Recommendations | "We recommend...", "The optimal course of action is...", "Priority should be given to..." |
| Impact | "This will result in...", "Expected outcomes include...", "The projected impact is..." |
| Urgency | "Immediate action is required...", "Time-sensitive...", "Delaying would result in..." |

### Transition Phrases for Reports
- "Based on the data presented above..."
- "This finding suggests that..."
- "In contrast to expectations..."
- "The implications of this are significant because..."
- "Taking all factors into consideration..."

### Recommendation Phrases
- "We recommend proceeding with..."
- "The evidence supports..."
- "Prioritizing [X] would yield the greatest return..."
- "This approach is favored because..."
- "The risk-adjusted return indicates..."

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Burying the Lead
❌ Putting the conclusion on page 15.

✅ Lead with the answer. Support it with details.

### 2. Data Without Interpretation
❌ "Revenue was $5.2M in Q3."

✅ "Revenue was $5.2M in Q3, a 12% increase driven by the new product
launch. This exceeds our target by 8% and puts us on track for the
annual goal."

### 3. Vague Recommendations
❌ "We should improve our marketing."

✅ "Increase digital marketing spend by 20% ($50K) in Q4, focusing on
paid search and LinkedIn, to capture the growing demand in the
enterprise segment. Expected ROI: 3.2x within 6 months."

### 4. Too Long
❌ A 40-page report when 5 pages would do.

✅ Respect the reader's time. Use appendices for supporting detail.

### 5. No Visuals
❌ Walls of text and numbers.

✅ Use charts, tables, and diagrams to make data scannable.

### 6. Passive, Tentative Language
❌ "It could potentially be considered that perhaps we might want to
explore the possibility of..."

✅ "We recommend. The data supports. The expected outcome is."

## Before / After Examples

### Example 1: Executive Summary

**Before:**
> This report provides an overview of the Q3 performance metrics for
> the organization. Various departments have submitted their data and
> it has been compiled and analyzed by the analytics team. The report
> covers financial performance, operational metrics, and strategic
> initiatives.

**After:**
> Q3 revenue hit $5.2M (+12% QoQ), exceeding target by 8%.
>
> **Key drivers:** New product launch ($1.1M), enterprise sales
> expansion (+22%).
>
> **Key risk:** Customer churn increased to 5.1% (from 3.8%).
> Immediate retention program recommended.
>
> **Recommendation:** Approve $50K Q4 marketing increase to sustain
> momentum. Expected ROI: 3.2x.

### Example 2: Finding with Interpretation

**Before:**
> Employee satisfaction scores were 3.6 out of 5 in the annual survey.
> This is lower than last year's score of 3.9.

**After:**
> Employee satisfaction dropped from 3.9 to 3.6 (-7.7%), the lowest
> score in three years. The decline was concentrated in two areas:
>
> - **Work-life balance:** 3.1 (↓ from 3.7) — correlates with the
>   15% increase in overtime since the restructuring
> - **Career growth:** 3.2 (↓ from 3.8) — 62% of respondents cited
>   "limited advancement opportunities"
>
> **If unaddressed:** Based on industry benchmarks, satisfaction below
> 3.5 correlates with 2x higher turnover. At our current replacement
> cost ($15K/employee), this could add $450K in annual hiring costs.
